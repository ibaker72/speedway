# Sales Agent Playbook

**Who this is for:** Anyone selling our platform to local service businesses — dealerships, HVAC, plumbing, roofing, electrical, med spas, dental, law, real estate, landscaping, restoration, any business that lives on local demand.

**What we sell in one sentence:** A custom-built website that gets you found in Google *and* in AI answers, captures visitors through multiple purpose-built funnels, scores every lead by how ready they are to buy, and updates itself automatically every week — and you own all of it.

> Read Part 1 and Part 2 before your first call. Parts 3–9 are the field manual. Part 10 is non-negotiable.

---

# PART 1 — The Mental Model

Stop thinking of this as "we build websites." You will lose that sale to a $200 Wix guy every time.

We sell **three machines**. Learn to diagnose which one a prospect is missing, because that's the one they'll pay for.

### Machine 1 — Visibility
Getting found by people who are already looking. Three layers, and they are not the same thing:
- **SEO** — ranking in traditional search results
- **GEO** — owning a separate page for each town/city you serve
- **AEO** — being the business an AI names when someone asks it for a recommendation

### Machine 2 — Conversion
Turning a visitor into a name and a phone number. Multiple capture paths for multiple mindsets — not one contact form.

### Machine 3 — Intelligence & Automation
Knowing which lead to call first, and having the whole system maintain itself on a schedule with nobody clicking anything.

**The diagnostic:**

| What they say | Machine they're missing | Lead your pitch with |
|---|---|---|
| "Nobody finds us / we're not on Google" | 1 — Visibility | GEO pages + structured data |
| "We get traffic but nobody calls" | 2 — Conversion | Capture flows + speed + trust signals |
| "We get leads but they're junk / we can't keep up" | 3 — Intelligence | Lead scoring + instant alerts + workflow |
| "Our site is 6 years old and we can't edit it" | Ownership | Custom build, you own it, no vendor lock |
| "We're spending a fortune on [Angi / CarGurus / Yelp / Thumbtack]" | Economics | Rent vs. own — this is the easiest sale of all |

**The single most powerful move in this playbook:** before every call, open ChatGPT and ask it the question their customer would ask — *"best HVAC company in [their city]"*, *"where can I sell my car near [their city]"*, *"emergency plumber [their city]"*. Screenshot the result. If they're not in it, that screenshot opens your call. If they are in it, congratulate them and pivot to conversion. Either way you've done homework nobody else did.

# PART 2 — SEO vs. AEO vs. GEO (Know This Cold)

You will get asked. Fumbling this costs you the deal. Learn the customer-language version, not the technical one.

### SEO — Search Engine Optimization
**Customer version:** "Making sure Google can read your site and decide you deserve to be on page one."

**What we actually do:** clean crawlable HTML, canonical URLs so pages don't compete with each other, auto-generated sitemap that includes every new page/product/listing the day it goes live, fast server-rendered pages so Google sees finished content instead of having to run JavaScript, proper heading structure, image optimization, internal linking.

**The line that lands:** *"Most of SEO isn't tricks. It's the plumbing being right. Your current site is probably failing three or four basic checks that take Google about a second to notice."*

---

### GEO — Geographic / Local Pages
**Customer version:** "A real page for every town you serve, so when someone two towns over searches, you actually show up."

**Why it works:** someone in Newark does not search "used cars Paterson." Someone in Naperville does not search "HVAC repair Chicago." They search *their own town*. If you don't have a page built for that exact search, you are invisible for it — no matter how good your homepage is.

**What we build:** an individual page per city with its own URL, its own keyword set, its own locally-written copy, its own FAQs answering local questions, its own local-business structured data with that city's name in it, and live content pulled from the business (inventory, services, jobs completed). Genuinely different per city — *not* find-and-replace, because Google filters duplicate city pages out and you get nothing.

**The line that lands:** *"You serve twelve towns. You have one page. That's one lottery ticket for twelve drawings."*

> ⚠️ Confusing overlap: in AI/marketing circles "GEO" now also means **Generative Engine Optimization** — optimizing to be cited by AI. If a prospect uses it that way, roll with it, and use **AEO** for the AI meaning so nobody gets lost.

---

### AEO — Answer Engine Optimization
**Customer version:** "When somebody asks ChatGPT or Google's AI 'who's the best [your trade] near me,' being the name that comes back."

**Why it's urgent right now:** search behavior is splitting. A growing share of people ask an assistant instead of scrolling ten blue links. An AI answer names two or three businesses. There is no page two. You're either in the answer or you don't exist for that customer.

**What we actually do:**
- **Structured data (JSON-LD)** — a machine-readable file on every page stating exactly what the business is, where, hours, phone, services, service area, rating and review count. This is the raw material AI engines and Google's map results consume.
- **Entity consistency** — name, address, phone identical everywhere, so the machine is confident it's one real business.
- **Question-and-answer content** marked up as `FAQPage` schema — AI engines pull directly from clean Q&A pairs.
- **Specific, factual, citable copy** — "we serve Passaic County and 24 surrounding cities, established 2005, 4.8 stars from 120+ reviews" beats "we're the area's premier provider." Vague marketing prose is uncitable.

**The line that lands:** *"Your site is written for humans to admire. It needs to also be written for machines to quote. Those are two different jobs and right now you're only doing one."*

---

### The 20-second version, if they ask you to just explain it fast

> "SEO is Google finding you. GEO is Google finding you in *every town you serve* instead of just the one you're sitting in. AEO is ChatGPT and Google's AI naming you when somebody asks for a recommendation. They're three different systems and most sites are built for none of them."

# PART 3 — The Call Flow

**Total: 45 minutes.** Never open with the product.

| # | Phase | Time | Objective |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Frame | 1 min | Get permission to run the call |
| 2 | Discover | 12 min | Find the bleeding, get the numbers |
| 3 | Play back | 2 min | Repeat their pain in their words — they must agree |
| 4 | Demo | 15 min | Show only what maps to what they said |
| 5 | Math | 5 min | Turn their numbers into a payback period |
| 6 | Objections | 5 min | Handle, then question back |
| 7 | Close | 5 min | One specific next step with a date |

**Frame (say this every time):**
> "I've got 45 blocked. First ten I'm asking about how you get customers today. Then I'll show you a live system and you tell me which pieces matter for you. If it's not a fit I'll say so and we both get our afternoon back. Fair?"

**Play back (this is the step agents skip and it's the most important one):**
> "Let me make sure I've got it. You're spending [$X] a month on [lead source], your own site gives you almost nothing, and when a lead does come in it sits in an inbox until somebody gets to it. That about right?"

They say yes → you now have permission to solve it. They correct you → even better, you just got the real problem.

# PART 4 — Discovery Question Bank

### Universal — ask these every call
1. "Walk me through how a customer finds you today."
2. "Percentage-wise, where do your leads come from?"
3. "What are you spending a month on [Angi / Thumbtack / CarGurus / Yelp / Google Ads]?"
4. "How many leads a month come off your own website? Are they good?"
5. "When a lead comes in — literally, what happens? Who sees it, how fast?"
6. "How fast do you call a new lead? Minutes, hours, next day?"
7. "What's a customer worth to you? Average job or average deal."
8. "How many of those do you close out of ten leads?"
9. "What towns do you actually serve?"
10. "Who built your site? Do you own it or rent it?"
11. "Could you change something on it tomorrow yourself?"
12. "What's the single most frustrating thing about your marketing?"
13. "If we're talking a year from now and you're thrilled — what happened?"
14. "If this makes sense, who else is in the decision?"

### Money questions — never skip these
You cannot build ROI without: **average job value**, **close rate**, **current monthly marketing spend**, **current lead volume**. Get all four. If they won't give real numbers, ask for ranges. If they won't give ranges, they're not qualified yet.

### Sharpeners — the ones that create urgency
- "Do you know how many people visit your site each month and leave without contacting you?" *(Almost always: no. That's your point.)*
- "Have you ever asked ChatGPT who the best [trade] in [city] is?" *(Then show your screenshot.)*
- "How many towns do you serve, and how many pages does your site have for them?"
- "If [Angi/CarGurus/Yelp] doubled their price next month, what happens to your business?"
- "How many leads do you think you've lost because nobody called them back within an hour?"

# PART 5 — Vertical Playbooks

Same platform, different vocabulary. Learn their language or you sound like a vendor.

---

## 🚗 Auto Dealerships

**Their pain:** renting leads from CarGurus/Cars.com/Autotrader at brutal cost; stale listings for sold units; no idea which lead is real; acquiring inventory is harder than selling it.

**Their money math:** front-end gross per unit × units per month.

**The hook:** *"You're paying CarGurus to introduce you to a buyer who's simultaneously being introduced to four other dealers. What if that search landed on your site instead?"*

**GEO angle:** a page per surrounding town. Buyers travel 20–40 minutes for the right car but they search their own town first.

**AEO angle:** vehicle-level structured data — VIN, price, mileage, in-stock status — so individual cars are eligible for automotive rich results and AI shopping answers.

**Automation angle:** daily inventory sync — new units live automatically, sold units pulled automatically. Kills the #1 complaint their customers have.

**The killer feature for them:** engagement scoring. A shopper who ran the payment calculator four times and asked for a test drive is not the same lead as a form-filler, and right now they're treated identically.

**The second killer feature:** a dedicated *sell your car / trade appraisal* funnel — an acquisition channel that competes with Carvana and CarMax for the same search, at zero cost per unit once built.

**KPI they care about:** cost per sold unit, days on lot.

---

## 🔧 HVAC / Plumbing / Electrical

**Their pain:** Angi and Thumbtack sell the same lead to four contractors and charge for junk; emergency calls go to whoever answers first; brutal seasonality.

**Their money math:** average ticket ($400 service call → $8–15k system replacement) × jobs per month.

**The hook:** *"You're paying Angi $80 for a lead they also sold to three of your competitors. Then you race them to the phone. What if that customer found you directly?"*

**GEO angle:** the single highest-ROI vertical for this. Every town in their service radius gets a page: "AC repair in [town]," "emergency plumber [town]," "water heater installation [town]." These are pure intent searches with a customer holding a credit card.

**AEO angle:** *"Emergency plumber near me"* is increasingly an AI-answered question. Structured data with 24/7 availability, service area, license number and review rating is what gets you named.

**Conversion angle:** emergency intake form + click-to-call that's impossible to miss on mobile — most of their traffic is a panicked person on a phone. Separate flows for emergency, scheduled maintenance, quote request, and financing.

**Automation angle:** instant lead alerts by text/email. In the trades, **speed to lead** is the entire game — the first contractor to call back wins a large majority of the time.

**Seasonal angle:** automated weekly content refresh means summer pages talk about AC and winter pages talk about heating without anyone rewriting them.

**KPI they care about:** booked jobs per month, cost per booked job.

---

## 🏠 Roofing / Siding / Windows / Restoration

**Their pain:** long sales cycle, huge ticket, storm-chaser competition, leads that ghost after the estimate.

**Their money math:** $8k–$25k per job. **One extra job pays for the entire platform.** Say that out loud — it's the easiest ROI close in this playbook.

**The hook:** *"At your average job size, this needs to produce one extra job a year to break even. Do you think a site that shows up in twelve towns instead of one produces one extra job a year?"*

**GEO angle:** storm and neighborhood targeting — "roof replacement in [town]," "storm damage repair [county]."

**Conversion angle:** free inspection request, financing pre-qualification, photo-upload damage assessment, insurance-claim assistance form. Four different mindsets, four funnels.

**Trust angle:** structured data carrying license number, insurance, warranty terms, review rating. High-ticket home services live and die on trust signals.

**KPI:** cost per estimate booked, estimate-to-close rate.

---

## 💆 Med Spa / Dental / Chiropractic / Veterinary

**Their pain:** no-shows, front desk drowning, competing on price against a chain, patients who research forever before booking.

**Their money math:** patient lifetime value, not first visit. A dental patient is worth thousands over years; a med spa membership is recurring.

**The hook:** *"You're not buying a website, you're buying [X] more new patients a month. At [$Y] lifetime value that's [$Z] a year."*

**GEO angle:** "[treatment] in [town]" — "Invisalign in [town]," "Botox near [town]." Patients travel 15 minutes maximum and search their own town.

**AEO angle:** enormous here. Patients ask AI *"is [treatment] worth it,"* *"how much does [treatment] cost,"* *"best [specialist] near me."* FAQ schema answering real treatment questions is exactly what gets pulled into those answers.

**Conversion angle:** consultation booking, treatment-specific inquiry forms, financing/membership signup, new patient intake.

**Compliance note:** healthcare has advertising rules and privacy obligations (HIPAA in the US). Never promise outcomes, never handle patient health information through a plain contact form without checking requirements first. **Flag anything HIPAA-adjacent to the technical team before you promise it.**

**KPI:** new patients per month, cost per new patient.

---

## ⚖️ Law Firms

**Their pain:** the most expensive keywords in existence, brutal competition, referral-dependent, one case can be worth six figures.

**Their money math:** average case value × cases per month. Personal injury: one case can exceed the entire annual platform cost by 10×.

**The hook:** *"One case. That's the entire conversation. What's your average case worth?"*

**GEO angle:** "[practice area] attorney [town]" — every town in the jurisdiction.

**AEO angle:** people ask AI legal questions constantly — *"do I need a lawyer for a car accident,"* *"how long do I have to file."* Structured Q&A content on those exact questions is how you get named as the firm to call.

**Conversion angle:** case evaluation form, free consultation booking, urgency-tiered intake.

**Compliance note:** bar advertising rules vary by state. No guarantees of outcome, no comparative superiority claims without substantiation, disclaimers required in many jurisdictions. **Do not draft legal marketing copy without their compliance sign-off.**

**KPI:** signed cases per month, cost per signed case.

---

## 🏡 Real Estate / Property Management

**Their pain:** Zillow owns their leads and sells them back; listings go stale; agents differentiated by nothing.

**The hook:** *"Zillow is charging you for leads generated by your own listings."*

**GEO angle:** neighborhood pages — "homes for sale in [neighborhood]," "[town] market report."

**Automation angle:** MLS/feed sync (structurally identical to the inventory sync we built for automotive — same machine, different data).

**Conversion angle:** home valuation request, buyer consultation, listing alerts, showing request.

**KPI:** listings taken, buyer-side transactions.

---

## 🍽️ Restaurants / Local Retail / Gyms

**Their pain:** thin margins, DoorDash fees, everything happens on Google Maps and Instagram.

**Honesty note:** this is our lowest-fit vertical for the full platform. Their economics often don't support a custom build. **Qualify hard or refer out.** If they have multiple locations, catering/events revenue, or memberships, it works. A single-location café usually does not — and telling them so buys you enormous credibility for the referral they'll send you later.

**If it does fit:** location pages per branch, catering/event inquiry funnel, membership signup, `LocalBusiness` and menu structured data.

---

## 🏢 B2B / Professional Services

**Their pain:** long cycles, hard attribution, marketing feels like a black hole.

**The hook:** *"You don't need more traffic, you need to know which five companies visited your pricing page this week."*

**Angle:** the intelligence machine is the whole pitch. Engagement scoring, source attribution, and lead workflow matter far more than city pages here.

# PART 6 — The ROI Math (Do It Live, On The Call)

Never present price without doing this first. Use *their* numbers, out loud, so they build the case themselves.

**The script:**
> "Let's do quick math with your numbers, not mine.
>
> You said your average [job/deal/case] is worth **[$A]**.
> You said you close about **[B]** out of every ten leads.
> So ten leads is roughly **[$A × B]** in revenue to you.
>
> This costs **[$C] a month.**
>
> So the question isn't whether this is expensive. The question is whether it produces **[C ÷ (A × B/10)] extra leads a month.** Round that up — call it [N] extra leads a month to break even. Everything past that is profit.
>
> You're currently getting [X] leads a month off a site that has one page for [number] towns and no structured data at all. Do you think [N] more a month is realistic?"

**Worked examples to have memorized:**

| Vertical | Avg value | Close rate | Break-even |
|---|---|---|---|
| HVAC | $1,200 ticket | 40% | ~2 extra leads/mo at $1k/mo |
| Roofing | $12,000 job | 25% | **1 job every 4 months** |
| Dealership | $2,200 front gross | 15% | ~3 extra units/mo |
| Med spa | $3,500 LTV | 30% | ~1 new patient/mo |
| PI law | $25,000/case | 10% | **1 case a year** |

**The rent-vs-own reframe (use it every time they flinch at price):**
> "You're spending [$X] a month on [Angi/CarGurus/Yelp]. That's [$X × 12] a year, and on the day you stop paying, it all stops — you keep nothing. This is [$Y] to build something you own that keeps working. In twelve months, one of those two is an asset and the other is a receipt."

# PART 7 — Objection Library

> **Rule: handle, then ask a question back.** Never let the call die on your answer.

**"It's too expensive."**
> "Compared to what? You're at [$X]/month on [lead source] with nothing to show at the end. What number would make this obviously worth it to you?"

**"I already have a website."**
> "You do. I looked at it before this call — [specific finding]. The question isn't whether you have a site, it's whether it's getting found and capturing. Right now it's doing neither. What did you pay for it?"

**"I need to think about it."**
> "Of course. What specifically — the money, the timing, or whether it'll work? Those are three different conversations and I can probably help with the one you're stuck on."

**"Send me information."**
> "Happy to. What would need to be in it for you to say yes? I'd rather send the two pages that answer that than a deck you'll skim."

**"I need to talk to my partner."**
> "Makes sense. When are you two talking? Let's get 20 minutes on the calendar with all three of us — otherwise you'll end up pitching this for me and I'll have given you the hard version to defend."

**"How long until results?"**
> "Different pieces, different timelines, and I'll be straight with you. Day one: site's live, faster, capturing through multiple funnels. Week one: structured data indexed, city pages live. Local rankings: 60–120 days for real movement. Anyone promising 30 days is lying. The reason I'm comfortable saying that is the capture and lead-scoring pieces pay for themselves while the SEO matures."

**"Can you guarantee page one?"**
> "No, and run from anyone who does — Google doesn't sell rankings. What I guarantee is that every technical thing Google and the AI engines say they want is implemented correctly and maintained automatically. Most of your competitors don't do that. That's the controllable part."

**"My guy handles our marketing."**
> "Keep him. He's doing posting and ads — real work, different discipline. I'm doing infrastructure: structured data, automated content, lead scoring, feed sync. He'd have more to work with, not less."

**"We're too busy / booked out right now."**
> "Best possible time. Everybody builds when they're desperate, which is the worst time — it takes [X weeks] to build and 60–120 days to rank, so you're planting for six months from now. What happens to your calendar in [their slow season]?"

**"We tried SEO before and it didn't work."**
> "What did they actually do? [Listen.] Yeah — that's usually blog posts and backlinks with no infrastructure underneath. Different thing. Did they ever build you a page per town, or add structured data? [Almost always no.] That's the part that was missing."

**"What if I want to leave?"**
> "You own the code, the database, the domain, and every lead record. It's standard technology any developer can pick up. That's deliberate — it's the opposite of what your current vendor is doing to you."

**"Is AI-written content going to get me penalized?"**
> "Google's position is about *unhelpful* content, not automated content — their own guidance says the production method isn't the issue, usefulness is. Two things keep us clean: it's grounded in your real facts, not invented, and every page runs an automated quality check before it can publish. It's not spraying junk pages, it's keeping [N] real pages current."

**"Can I just get the website part?"**
> "You can, and I'll build it. But understand what you're buying — the site alone is the brochure. The reason this works is the three parts together: found, captured, scored. If budget's the issue let's talk about phasing it instead of gutting it. What's the number you're working with?"

**"Do you have case studies?"**
> "[Show what you genuinely have — the live Speedway platform, features working in production. Do not invent metrics. If you don't have permission to share a client's numbers, say so:] I can't share another client's private numbers without their sign-off, but I can walk you through the live system and you can judge the work yourself."

**Silence after your price.**
> Say nothing. Count to ten in your head. Let them speak first. Every time.

# PART 8 — Pricing & Packaging

**Never quote before discovery.** If they push early:
> "I'll give you a real number, and I want to — but if I quote before I know whether you need four city pages or twelve, and whether you've got a clean data feed or we're building the import from scratch, I'm just making one up. Two more questions and I can price it accurately."

**Structure:** one-time build + monthly platform fee.
The monthly covers hosting, the automated jobs, AI content generation, monitoring, and support. Frame it as **operations, not maintenance** — "this is what keeps the machine running every week," not "this is in case something breaks."

**Present three tiers, always.** Nobody buys the cheapest when there are three, and the top tier makes the middle look reasonable.

| | Foundation | Growth *(most pick this)* | Dominate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Custom site + full SEO infrastructure | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| Structured data / AEO | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| City landing pages | 3–4 | 8–12 | 15+ |
| Lead capture funnels | 2 | All | All |
| Lead dashboard + engagement scoring | — | ✅ | ✅ |
| Instant lead alerts | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| Automated data/inventory sync | — | ✅ | ✅ |
| Weekly AI content refresh | — | ✅ | ✅ |
| Analytics + source attribution | Basic | Full | Full |
| Support | Email | Priority | Priority + monthly strategy call |

→ Anchor high. Present Dominate first, then land on Growth. Put "most clients choose this" on Growth and say it out loud.

**When they negotiate:** never cut price without cutting scope. Drop city pages from 12 to 6. Phase the Deal Desk to month two. A discount with no scope change teaches them the first number was fake.

# PART 10 — Non-Negotiables

**Never do these. They end deals and they end careers.**

❌ **Never invent a number.** No made-up traffic lifts, ranking gains, or revenue figures. Ever. If you don't have a real number, describe the capability instead. "Here's what the system does" is a strong pitch. A fabricated statistic is a lawsuit and a dead reputation.

❌ **Never guarantee rankings.** Google doesn't sell them and doesn't take requests. Guarantee *work*, not *outcomes*.

❌ **Never share a client's private performance data** without written permission. Public, live features on a public site are fair game — their conversion rates are not.

❌ **Never promise a feature that doesn't exist.** If they ask for something we don't have, say: *"That's not in the platform today. Let me check with the team on what it'd take to build."* Then actually check. Selling vapor makes the delivery team eat it and makes the client fire you.

❌ **Never trash a competitor by name.** Point at facts on their site. Let the prospect draw the conclusion.

❌ **Never quote a price you're not authorized to quote.** Come back with a real number instead of guessing.

❌ **Never skip compliance in regulated verticals.** Medical, legal, and financial services have advertising rules. Flag them before you promise anything.

**Always do these:**

✅ Do the pre-call homework — the AI screenshot, the site audit, the competitor check. It's 15 minutes and it's the whole difference.
✅ Ask more than you talk. Target 70/30 in discovery.
✅ Write down their exact words and use them back.
✅ Do the ROI math live, with their numbers.
✅ Give one specific next step with a date.
✅ Tell them when it's not a fit. The referrals from honest no's outnumber the deals you'd have forced.
